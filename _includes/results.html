Discussion

We evaluated our model on both synthetic and real-world datasets for cases with binary and multi-class observed labels. For binary data, our results show that the model effectively reduces demographic parity difference while maintaining similar Area Under the Curve (AUC) and accuracy scores. This is most evident in our synthetic dataset, where the model successfully removed the injected bias, aligning AUC, accuracy, and demographic parity difference with those of the original unbiased data. However, for real-world datasets, since we assume complete independence from the sensitive feature, there is a slight trade-off between demographic parity difference and AUC/accuracy. For multi-class data, the model remains effective, but the trade-off between demographic parity and AUC/accuracy is more pronounced. In the synthetic dataset, the model reduced the demographic parity difference compared to the biased dataset, though it did not fully reach the fairness level of the original dataset. Additionally, accuracy declined when compared to the original fair data. A similar trend was observed in the Drug Consumption dataset, emphasizing the need for further improvements to enhance performance in multi-class scenarios. Overall, this model helps mitigate bias between the observed label and sensitive features, ensuring that outcomes are not unfairly influenced or discriminatory. Future work will focus on testing the model in a wider range of scenarios, including cases where the sensitive feature itself is multi-class.
